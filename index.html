Food Week

When during the work week do Americans spend money on food, and why? Ask a question and a team of AI agents digs through five US datasets (federal spending diaries, food surveys, time-use diaries and NYC subway data) to answer it with cited numbers.

What it explores

Which weekdays people spend the most on groceries and eating out, who differs (workers, retirees, New Yorkers), and whether hybrid work, end-of-week habits or paydays explain it.

Who it's for

Café and restaurant owners planning staff and promotions, reporters covering hybrid work, and anyone curious about everyday spending habits.

How to use it

Ask about Monday-Friday food spending or eating. A lead agent plans, researchers query the data in parallel, and a writer reports. It takes 1-3 minutes. Vague questions get a follow-up question.

The data, and its limits
  • BLS Consumer Expenditure Diary, 2015-2025: household food dollars by weekday, with the New York metro area.
  • Atlanta Fed Diary of Consumer Payment Choice, 2021-2025 (October): individual food dollars by day, by employment.
  • CDC NHANES, 2017-2023: where people's food came from (restaurant vs store) by weekday.
  • BLS American Time Use Survey, 2015-2025: food activities by weekday and by where people worked that day.
  • MTA subway ridership, 2017-now (live): the shape of the NYC office week.

It covers Monday-Friday only, can't see individual people's whole weeks, and can't prove why patterns happen. The report says when the data can't answer something.
