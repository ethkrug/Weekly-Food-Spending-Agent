Food Week

When does America
spend on food?

Ask about Monday–Friday groceries and eating out. Answers come from five US datasets, and every number links to the query that produced it.

What it exploresWhich weekdays people spend the most on groceries and eating out, who differs (workers, retirees, New Yorkers), and whether hybrid work, end-of-week habits or paydays explain it.
Who it's forCafé and restaurant owners planning staff and promotions, reporters covering hybrid work, and anyone curious about everyday spending habits.
How to use itType a question or tap an example. A run takes 1–3 minutes and you can watch the research as it happens. Vague questions get a short follow-up question.
The data, and its limits
  • BLS Consumer Expenditure Diary, 2015–2025: household food dollars by weekday, including the New York metro area.
  • Atlanta Fed Diary of Consumer Payment Choice, October 2021–2025: a national sample (not just Atlanta) of individual food dollars by day, by employment.
  • CDC NHANES, 2017–2023: where people's food came from (restaurant vs store) by weekday.
  • BLS American Time Use Survey, 2015–2025: food activities by weekday and by where people worked that day.
  • MTA subway ridership, 2017–now (live): the shape of the NYC office week.

It covers Monday–Friday only, can't follow one person through a whole week, and can't prove why patterns happen. The report says when the data can't answer something.
